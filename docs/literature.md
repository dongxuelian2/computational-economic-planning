# Initial literature and data sources

## Computational economic coordination

1. Ricardo Alonzo Fernández Salguero (2026), **Testing Centralized and Polycentric Computational Planning**, arXiv:2606.19214. https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19214  
   Common synthetic benchmark for centralized, agent-based market, and hybrid coordination; useful for designing symmetric tests and stress scenarios.

2. Jiale Han et al. (2026), **From Economic Agents to Agentic Economies: A Systems Blueprint for Economic World Models**, arXiv:2608.06020. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06020  
   Research architecture for heterogeneous-agent simulations and empirically aligned economic world models.

## Data and calibration

3. OECD, **Inter-Country Input-Output Tables (ICIO)**. https://www.oecd.org/en/data/datasets/inter-country-input-output-tables.html  
   Cross-country sector-level economic linkages and historical accounts.

4. Eurostat, **ESA Supply, Use and Input-Output Tables / FIGARO**. https://ec.europa.eu/eurostat/en/web/esa-supply-use-input-tables/database  
   European supply-use and global inter-country input-output data.

5. **Chinese provincial and international multi-regional input-output dataset** (public dataset and accompanying code). https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28973396.v3  
   Potential foundation for province-level network experiments. Verify dataset schema, price base, and reuse conditions before importing.

## Literature workflow

For each paper, record the mathematical model, information assumptions, objective function, available data/code, main mathematical result, limitations, and a concrete extension hypothesis. Keep speculative directions separate from established results.
